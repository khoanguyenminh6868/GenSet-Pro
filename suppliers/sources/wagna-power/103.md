What Is Green Power Natural Gas Generator
A natural gas generator for home is a type of power generator that utilizes natural gas as its primary fuel source to produce electricity. Natural gas, a fossil fuel composed mainly of methane, is commonly extracted from underground reservoirs and distributed through pipelines. Natural gas generators harness this abundant and cleaner-burning fuel to generate electrical power, offering a reliable alternative to traditional diesel or gasoline generators.
Advantages of Green Power Natural Gas Generator
Reliably Backs Up Electricity
Natural gas backup generators provide peace of mind, ready to get to work if the power goes out. Backup generators monitor power around the clock. When generators detect an outage, they automatically switch on with little to no disruption in power.
Access To Fuel
Since natural gas is the most reliable energy source, it's a great fuel option for backup generators. Unlike diesel, propane or gasoline generators, you don't need to plan ahead. There's no need to buy fuel, figure out where to store fuel, or worry if fuel has gone bad.
Less Expensive To Operate
Natural gas is an affordable fuel source. Compared to diesel, propane or gasoline, natural gas generators cost significantly less to operate. Plus, we offer rebates on installation costs for natural gas generators.
Customizable Sizing
Natural gas backup generators are capable of powering a couple appliances or your whole home. Unlike portable generators, that only power an appliance or two, natural gas generators come in a variety of sizes. You can calculate your specific energy needs and install a generator that works best for you.
Better For The Environment
Natural gas is an environmentally friendly fuel choice. Compared to diesel, propane or gasoline, natural gas generators produce less greenhouse gasses. They're a responsible option to power your home in the event of an emergency.
Types of Green Power Natural Gas Generator
01.
Standby
Standby generators are common in both residential and commercial and industrial applications. In both cases, the natural gas generator is permanently installed and supplied with fuel from the local utility company. During power outages, the standby generator swings into operation automatically. It is only intended to run for a limited time during emergency periods, as operating a standby generator for more than its prescribed amount can cause malfunctions and the need for more frequent repairs.
Natural gas generators make an excellent choice for standby power because, while outages can happen, they are extremely rare for natural gas because the pipes are below ground and out of the elements. So, unless situated in an area susceptible to earthquakes, homeowners and business owners can rely on having an unlimited supply of generator fuel that doesn't "go bad" like diesel or have to be stored in tanks.
02.
Prime
Prime power generators are used when there is no local utility, hence the generated power is the only power source available. This type of natural gas generator is less common, especially for residential purposes, as most homes don't have the ability to quickly connect to a natural gas line, and many have no gas line at all. And without a natural gas hookup to a utility line, the natural gas must be delivered by truck.
Prime power generators have much longer running times and can handle larger loads than backup generators. When a commercial natural gas generator is needed on a job site, the generator is typically transported there on a trailer or fifth wheel.
A subset of prime power generators are continuous power generators. The two are similar except that continuous generator sets are meant to run continually with a consistent load, while prime generators are built to run for long periods at variable loads.
Components of a Green Power Natural Gas Generator
A natural gas generator is a complex system that converts the chemical energy stored in natural gas into electricity, and to accomplish this task, several key components work together:
01/
Engine
The heart of a natural gas generator is the internal combustion engine, which engine works similarly to a car engine, but is modified for continuous power generation. Natural gas engines are specifically designed to handle the properties of natural gas, such as its high methane content.
02/
Alternator
Also known as the generator end, the alternator is responsible for converting the rotational energy from the engine into electricity, and contains a rotor and a stator, and the interaction between a rotating magnetic field and a stationary coil induces a current.
03/
Fuel System
The fuel system is designed to deliver a controlled amount of natural gas to the engine combustion chambers which usually includes components such as gas regulators, fuel control valves and injectors. Modern natural gas generators often employ electronic fuel injection systems for precise control.
04/
Cooling System
Generators generate a lot of heat during operation and require a cooling system to maintain an optimal operating temperature with the system typically including a radiator, cooling fan, and thermostat.
05/
Exhaust System
The exhaust system directs the combustion gases away from the engine and may include catalytic converters to reduce emissions and ensure compliance with environmental regulations.
06/
Control Panel
The control panel contains the generator's control unit manages its various functions and is capable of monitoring parameters such as voltage, frequency, and load to ensure stable generator operation.
How Does a Green Power Natural Gas Generator Work?
A natural gas standby generator is also called a whole house generator or a whole home generator. It is a stand-alone system, run on natural gas, that's connected to your home. When the power goes out, the generator comes on, automatically. A separate device, called an Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS) isolates your home from the power grid once the generator reaches full speed. That will protect you and the grid from power surges. When the power comes back on, you'll switch back over to the local utility grid for electricity.
Since the whole home generator has a continuous natural gas supply, you don't have to worry about purchasing and storing gasoline for a portable generator. And since it's attached to your main electrical system, you don't have to run extension cords.
Cost is usually the biggest factor when determining if you want a whole house generator or a portable generator.
Green Power Natural Gas Generator Maintenance: How to Be Prepared
If you knew you were going to be in an environment with a high risk of getting a cold, would you take medicine that treats colds, or medicine that prevents them? Of course you'd want to prevent them! It's the same with the maintenance of a natural gas generator. Although natural gas generators can last anywhere between 10,000 to 30,000 hours, they require maintenance to keep them running smoothly and to prevent any breakdowns.
Weekly
Run the generator in order to ensure that the unit works and there are no alarms or warning signals. Check the fuel level, that the circuit breaker is closed, and no fluid is leaking. Make sure the generator is in "auto" mode for automatic startup.
Monthly
Check the level of the engine coolant, oil, and the battery charger.
Routine visual inspection to ensure: The generator and its surroundings are clean (even if the unit is enclosed, make sure no rodents made it their home), there are no loose wires or clamps, there is no corrosion to wires or the battery, and that everything functions properly.
Bi-annual
Inspect the enclosure, drive belts, coolant heater, exhaust system, air induction piping and connections, the dc electrical system, and the ac wiring and accessories. Examine the battery electrolyte level, specific gravity, cables, and connections. Check for coolant, oil, or fuel leaks, including their connectors and hoses. Examine and clean the air cleaner units.
Annual
Change the filters for the oil, fuel, and air, and change the oil as well.
Change the spark plugs. Clean the crankcase breather. Flush the cooling system and conduct load bank testing. Check the coolant concentration.
By following this straightforward checklist for your natural gas generator, you can ensure its long life and your peace of mind. All in all, by the end of each year the fuel system, coolant system, lubrication system, air system, starting system, alternator, and transfer switch should all have been inspected and serviced if necessary.
Extend the Life Expectancy of Your Green Power Natural Gas Generator
Check Engine Oil
Check the oil levels once a month and before starting your generator. Use the dipstick to check and fill it up to the optimal level if it's running low. Running a generator when it's low on oil will cause the engine to shut down. How often you need to change your oil depends on the generator brand, how often you use it and where you store it.
If there's a lot of dust and contaminants in the environment around your generator, you'll likely need to change the oil more often. If you're in an area that is colder that can face sub-zero temperatures, consider using oil that maintains viscosity when experiencing colder climates.
Check The Radiator And Coolant Level
After you've checked your oil, move on to your coolant. Checking and maintaining your coolant level is essential to keep your generator running for longer. The coolant prevents the generator from overheating when it's running. Remove the cap from your radiator only when your generator is cold - never check your coolant when your generator is still warm.
Look inside to see whether the coolant is above the radiator elements. If it's below the recommended level, it's time for a top-up. Slowly pour in the coolant until it reaches the marked level. Never add coolant to a running or warm generator, and only use the type that the manufacturer specifies. Check for leaks, too, that may come from loose pipes, the radiator or the ventilation fan.
Inspect Any Natural Gas Lines
As natural gas is highly flammable and toxic when exposed for longer durations, it's important to inspect any gas tubes and lines connected to the generator. Inspect the line for deformities, leakage or unstable pressure. If you notice any signs of leakage or strange smells, it's important to call a professional immediately. Working with natural gas comes with potential risks that a professional will know how to handle. They'll make sure to fix any issues professionally while following the law.
Check Gas Levels
There are instances where natural gas generators may be converted to run off of propane gas. If you have a natural gas to propane conversion, it's important to check your propane tank for any leaks, pressure issues, and most importantly – gas levels. In the event of an emergency or power outage, the last thing you would want is to be without gas to effectively run your backup generator.
Service Your Generator Regularly
If you want your generator to last, keep up with regular services. They keep your generator reliable and ensure it complies with new emissions regulations. Natural gas generators require more regular maintenance when compared to their diesel counterparts due to the fact that the engines are spark-ignited due to the spark plugs within the engine.
Regular services can save you both money and downtime - make sure you hire a specialist who knows exactly how to service your generator.
Check The Battery
One of the most common failures in a generator starting is, believe it or not – the battery. Your generator is very similar to a car - you need both fuel and a good battery to power it. Whether you're using your generator or not, the battery may drain and will decrease over time. Check the levels monthly - if it's too low, it can damage the battery by negatively impacting the maximum charging cycle and the battery can even go bad over time. When you don't use your generator for several days, that can also cause battery failure.
