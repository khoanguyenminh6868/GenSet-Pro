Most diesel generators take between 1 and 15 liters of oil, depending on their size: a small portable unit needs about 1–3 liters , a medium commercial generator 5–8 liters , and a large industrial diesel generator 10–15 liters . The exact amount depends on the engine size, type and design, so always confirm it against your manufacturer’s manual.
The right oil capacity 1 , the correct oil type and a consistent change schedule together keep the engine lubricated, cool and running efficiently for years. Below we cover how to determine your generator’s oil capacity, which oil to use and how often to change it.
How Do You Determine the Oil Capacity of a Generator?
The oil capacity of a generator depends on its size, type, and engine design. To determine the correct oil capacity, you’ll need to refer to the manufacturer’s manual or check the engine specifications 2 .
Here’s how you can determine the oil capacity for your generator:
Steps to Determine Oil Capacity
Consult the owner’s manual. The easiest and most reliable way to find the oil capacity is by checking the generator’s user manual or maintenance guide, which lists the exact oil capacity in liters or gallons for your specific model.
Check the engine’s oil dipstick. For some smaller portable generators, you can estimate the oil capacity by checking the dipstick after draining — though the manual is always the most accurate source.
Consider the engine size. Larger industrial diesel generators require more oil because of their bigger engines. These may take anywhere from 5 to 15 liters or more, while smaller generators might only need 1–3 liters.
Look for a label on the generator. Some generators have an oil capacity label near the engine or oil reservoir that states the exact amount required.
Typical Oil Capacity for Common Generator Sizes
| | Generator Type | Typical Power | Oil Capacity (Liters) | Oil Capacity (Quarts)
| Small Portable Diesel | 3–10 kW | 1–3 L | ≈ 1–3 qt
| Medium Commercial Diesel | 20–100 kW | 5–8 L | ≈ 5–8.5 qt
| Large Industrial Diesel | 150 kW and up | 10–15 L | ≈ 10.5–16 qt
How Much Oil Does a Generator Use Per Hour?
Oil capacity — how much you pour in — is different from oil consumption — how much the engine burns while running. A healthy diesel generator consumes very little oil between changes, typically well under 0.1% of its fuel consumption (roughly 0.5 g/kWh on a modern engine).
If you find yourself topping up the oil often between scheduled changes, that usually points to a real problem — worn piston rings, leaking seals, or a clogged crankcase breather. Have the engine inspected instead of simply adding more oil.
What Type of Oil Should Be Used in a Generator?
The type of oil to use in a generator depends on the engine specifications, operating temperature, and usage conditions. Using the correct oil helps maintain proper engine performance and prevents premature wear.
Generally, you should choose between mineral oils 3 , synthetic oils 4 , or semi-synthetic oils 5 based on your generator’s engine requirements.
Types of Oils for Diesel Generators
Mineral Oil — Best for older generators or moderate operating conditions. Cost-effective and lubricates well under normal conditions, but breaks down faster at high temperatures and can create more carbon buildup.
Synthetic Oil — Best for high-performance engines, cold-weather conditions and continuous operation. Lasts longer, lubricates better and performs well in extreme temperatures, reducing wear and improving fuel efficiency, but costs more.
Semi-Synthetic Oil — Best for most modern diesel engines. Balances performance and cost, with better protection than mineral oil at a lower price than full synthetic.
Multi-Grade Oil — Best for engines that run in varying temperatures, adjusting viscosity for both hot and cold conditions.
Recommended Oil Viscosity & API Rating
Match the SAE viscosity grade 6 to your climate, and use an API service category 7 rated for diesel engines (CJ-4 or the newer CK-4):
Warm climates: SAE 15W-40 or 20W-50
Cold climates: SAE 10W-30 or 5W-30
Heavy-duty use: SAE 40 or 50 for high-temperature operation
For modern diesel engines, API CK-4 oil offers better oxidation stability and soot control than the older CJ-4 spec, so prefer CK-4 when your engine allows it.
How Often Should You Change the Oil in a Generator?
Changing the oil regularly is crucial for keeping your generator running smoothly . Oil breaks down over time and loses its ability to lubricate effectively, leading to engine wear and potential failure.
The general recommendation for oil changes 8 varies depending on the type of generator and usage:
Recommended Oil Change Intervals
For new generators: change after the first 20–50 hours to remove manufacturing contaminants, then every 100–200 hours or 6 months (whichever comes first).
For regular use: change roughly every 100–150 hours of operation; inspect more often under heavy-duty use.
For infrequent use: change at least once a year — oil degrades over time even when the generator sits idle.
Before and after long-term storage: always change the oil to keep the engine lubricated and free from contaminants.
Oil Change Reminder
| | Usage Condition | Recommended Oil Change Interval
| New Generator | First change after 20–50 hours, then every 100–200 hours
| Regular Use | Every 100–150 hours or 6 months
| Infrequent Use | At least once a year
| Long-Term Storage | Change before and after storage
Conclusion
Knowing the oil capacity, selecting the right type of oil, and regularly changing the oil are essential for keeping your generator in peak condition. By following these guidelines, you can ensure your generator runs efficiently, lasts longer, and avoids costly repairs. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
How much oil does a generator need?
Most generators need 1–15 liters of oil depending on size: small portable units 1–3 liters, medium commercial generators 5–8 liters, and large industrial units 10–15 liters. Check the owner’s manual for your exact model.
How much oil do you put in a generator?
Fill to the dipstick’s full mark (or the manual’s specified capacity) with the recommended oil grade — typically 1–3 liters for portables, 5–8 liters for commercial units and 10–15 liters for large industrial generators.
How many quarts of oil does a generator take?
As a rough guide, a small portable takes about 1–3 quarts, a medium commercial unit about 5–8.5 quarts, and a large industrial generator about 10.5–16 quarts. (1 liter ≈ 1.06 quarts.)
How much oil does a generator use per hour?
Oil capacity is different from oil consumption. A healthy diesel generator should consume very little oil between changes — often under 0.1% of fuel consumption. If you’re topping up oil frequently, have the engine checked for leaks or wear.
What type of oil should I use in my generator?
Use a diesel-rated oil that matches your climate: SAE 15W-40 or 20W-50 in warm climates, and SAE 10W-30 or 5W-30 in cold climates. Semi-synthetic oils are a good all-round choice for most modern diesel engines.
How often should I change my generator oil?
Change it after the first 20–50 hours for a new generator, then every 100–200 hours or 6 months for regular use, and at least once a year for standby units. Change before and after long-term storage.
