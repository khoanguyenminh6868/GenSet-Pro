Conclusion
A diesel generator runs most efficiently at 70–80% of its rated load. Load is the power being drawn at any moment, expressed as a percentage of the generator’s rated capacity — running below ~30% causes wet stacking, while sustained 100% load causes overheating. Below we explain how to calculate it and manage it.
Managing load well keeps the generator running continuously and extends its life. Here is the relationship between load and performance, plus the formula to calculate it.
What Is the Relationship Between Load and Diesel Generator Performance?
Load is the amount of electrical power drawn from the generator, and it directly affects performance, fuel consumption and lifespan.
Impact of Load on Generator Performance
Fuel efficiency. Higher load means higher fuel use, but around 70–80% of rated load is the most fuel-efficient operating point.
Engine wear. Too low or too high a load causes excess wear — light load leads to incomplete combustion and carbon buildup; constant full load overheats the engine.
Performance and reliability. Moderate load (70–80%) keeps the engine stable without unnecessary strain.
Load variability. Load fluctuates as equipment turns on and off, and the generator adjusts its output.
Why Load Management Matters
Underloading (below ~30% of rated capacity) causes wet stacking 1 — unburned fuel building up in the exhaust.
Overloading (at or above maximum capacity for long periods) causes overheating, premature wear and possible failure.
How Is the Load on a Diesel Generator Calculated?
Load is calculated by comparing power output to the generator’s rated capacity, in kW or kVA. See also how to calculate kVA .
Steps to Calculate Load
Determine the generator’s rated power 2 from the manufacturer’s specifications (kW or kVA).
Measure the total power demand — sum the power ratings of all connected loads.
Calculate the load percentage — divide total demand by rated capacity and multiply by 100: load % = (total demand ÷ rated power) × 100 3 .
For example, a 100 kVA generator supplying 60 kVA runs at (60 ÷ 100) × 100 = 60% load .
| | Generator Rated Power | Total Power Demand (Load) | Load Percentage
| 100 kVA | 60 kVA | 60%
| 200 kVA | 180 kVA | 90%
| 500 kVA | 250 kVA | 50%
These examples show how the load percentage tracks the total demand against the rated power.
What Happens If a Diesel Generator Runs with Low or High Load?
Operating outside the optimal range reduces efficiency, increases fuel consumption and wears the engine faster.
Running at Low Load
Inefficient combustion. Under 30% load, the engine doesn’t burn fuel fully, causing wet stacking and carbon buildup.
Poor fuel economy. More fuel is consumed per unit of electricity.
Shortened engine life. Incomplete combustion wears pistons and rings.
Reduced reliability. Uneven wear raises maintenance costs.
Running at High Load
Overheating. Constant near-full load overheats the engine, especially with inadequate cooling.
Increased wear. More friction and higher temperatures wear bearings, pistons and the fuel system.
Risk of overloading. Exceeding rated capacity trips or damages the generator.
Higher fuel consumption. Full load is costly even though 70–80% is efficient.
How to Manage Load Effectively
Avoid prolonged low load (below 30%).
Keep load at 70–80% for the best balance of performance, fuel efficiency and engine life.
Use load bank testing if the generator rarely runs at full load. For correct sizing, see what size generator you need .
Frequently Asked Questions
What is load on a diesel generator?
Load is the electrical power drawn from the generator, usually expressed as a percentage of its rated capacity.
What is the best load percentage for a diesel generator?
70–80% of rated load gives the best balance of fuel efficiency, performance and engine life.
How do I calculate generator load percentage?
Load % = (total power demand ÷ generator rated power) × 100. For example, 60 kVA on a 100 kVA set is 60% load.
What happens if a diesel generator runs at low load?
Below about 30% load the engine doesn’t burn fuel fully, causing wet stacking, carbon buildup and shorter engine life.
What happens if a generator runs at 100% load?
Sustained full load causes overheating, accelerated wear and the risk of an overload trip.
What is wet stacking?
Wet stacking is unburned fuel collecting in the exhaust from prolonged low-load operation.
Conclusion
Load directly affects a diesel generator’s performance, fuel efficiency and lifespan. Keeping it at 70–80% of rated capacity, calculating it correctly and avoiding prolonged low load ensures the generator runs at peak efficiency for years.
