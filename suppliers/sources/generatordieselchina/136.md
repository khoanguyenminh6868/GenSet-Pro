A 32 A load is 7.36 kVA on a 230 V single-phase supply — about 5.9 kW at 0.8 power factor — and 22.2 kVA on a 400 V three-phase supply. Because a 32 A circuit is treated as a continuous load, the set is sized at 125% of that figure: a 10 kVA single-phase generator or a 30 kVA three-phase generator. Those two cases cover most installations; the table below also sizes 120 V and 480 V supplies, which land at 5 kVA and 40 kVA.
The table below covers 120 V, 208 V, 230 V, 240 V, 400/415 V and 480 V systems, so you can check the figures for your own supply. Every size follows the same method, and the diesel generator range these numbers point to runs from 30 kW to 2,000 kW.
What size generator do I need for 32 amps?
Two things move the answer: the supply voltage, and whether the 32 A figure is a measured load or the rating of a socket. The table assumes 32 A at 0.8 power factor and applies the 125% continuous-duty margin; a purely resistive load can be sized on the load kVA alone.
Generator size for a 32 A load by supply voltage (80% continuous-duty rule) | | System | Voltage | Load kVA | Load kW (0.8 PF) | Sized kVA (80% rule) | Recommended generator
| Single-phase | 120 V | 3.84 | 3.07 | 4.8 | 5 kVA
| Single-phase | 230 V | 7.36 | 5.89 | 9.2 | 10 kVA
| Single-phase | 240 V | 7.68 | 6.14 | 9.6 | 10 kVA
| Three-phase | 208 V | 11.53 | 9.22 | 14.4 | 15 kVA
| Three-phase | 400 V | 22.17 | 17.74 | 27.7 | 30 kVA
| Three-phase | 480 V | 26.60 | 21.28 | 33.3 | 40 kVA
The last column is the standard rating to order. An 8 kVA set carries 32 A at 230 V at 92% loading, outside the 80% band; the 10 kVA unit is what brings it back inside.
How do you convert 32 amps to kVA and kW?
Current becomes power in one step. Apparent power (kVA) comes directly from amps and volts; real power (kW) then applies the power factor.
Single-phase: kVA = amps × volts ÷ 1,000
Three-phase: kVA = amps × volts × 1.732 ÷ 1,000
Real power: kW = kVA × power factor
At 230 V single-phase, 32 × 230 ÷ 1,000 = 7.36 kVA , which is 5.9 kW at 0.8 power factor. At 400 V three-phase, 32 × 400 × 1.732 ÷ 1,000 = 22.2 kVA , or 17.7 kW at the same power factor. At 480 V three-phase the same current gives 26.6 kVA.
Generating sets are rated under ISO 8528-5 1 at a 0.8 power factor reference, so the kVA figure is the one to compare with a nameplate; the kW figure tells you how much of that capacity does useful work.
If you would rather work back from a load list, our guide on how to calculate kVA for a generator runs the same arithmetic for mixed loads.
Does the load type change the generator size?
It changes the margin you need, not the base arithmetic. Resistive loads such as heaters and incandescent lighting run at a power factor close to 1.0 and draw almost no reactive current. Motors, compressors, pumps and air conditioners are inductive: their power factor sits around 0.6–0.8, and their starting current is several times the running current.
| | Load type | Power factor | Effect on generator sizing
| Resistive | Close to 1.0 (lighting, heating) | Size on the load kVA; almost no starting surge.
| Inductive | 0.6–0.8 (motors, compressors) | Add starting margin; a direct-on-line motor may need 2–3× its running kW.
| Capacitive | Close to 1.0 (capacitor banks) | Similar to resistive, but can disturb voltage regulation.
The 80% rule covers the continuous part. A load that runs for more than three hours counts as continuous, and continuous loads are limited to 80% of the circuit rating 2 — the same 125% margin applied in the table above. Motor starting is a separate question: a direct-on-line motor can pull two to three times its running kW for a second or two, and that is what decides whether you need a larger set or a soft starter.
For the physics behind reactive current, see generator load and power factor .
Single-phase or three-phase at 32 amps?
This is where most sizing mistakes happen. Three-phase does not make the generator smaller. For the same power in kW, a single-phase and a three-phase set need the same kVA rating; what changes is the current in each conductor.
At the same 32 A, a 400 V three-phase supply delivers 22.2 kVA while a 230 V single-phase supply delivers 7.36 kVA, because all three phases carry 32 A. That is three times the power at the same current, so the three-phase set is the larger one, not the smaller. The reason to choose three-phase is the lower current per conductor: smaller cables, lower voltage drop and easier motor starting.
| | Factor | Single-phase | Three-phase
| Typical voltage | 120 V or 230 V | 400 V or 480 V
| Power delivery | One alternating wave | Three waves, 120° apart
| Current per conductor | Full load current in one conductor | The same current in each of three conductors
| Generator size | Same kVA for the same kW load | Same kVA for the same kW load; three times the kVA for the same 32 A
| Best fit | Smaller loads, domestic and light commercial | Motors, long cable runs, 30 kVA and above
If you are sizing a whole service rather than one circuit, the same method scales up — see sizing for a 200 amp service .
What is a 32 amp generator?
A “32 amp generator” is not a size class. It is a set fitted with a 32 A IEC 60309 3 industrial socket — the blue 3-pin outlet for 230 V single-phase used across the UK, Europe and Australia, or the red 5-pin outlet for 400/415 V three-phase. In North America the equivalent outlet is the 30 A NEMA L14-30 or TT-30, so the same class of set is sold there as “30 amp”.
The socket does not set the size. What matters is that the set can supply 32 A continuously at the socket voltage: 7.36 kVA at 230 V. An 8 kVA set is the smallest that can carry a 32 A plug at all, and 10 kVA is the one that carries it with margin.
Conclusion
To size a generator for a 32 A load, work out the kVA at your voltage and phase — 7.36 kVA at 230 V single-phase, 22.2 kVA at 400 V three-phase — then size at 125% for continuous duty: a 10 kVA single-phase set or a 30 kVA three-phase set. Use the power factor only to convert kVA into kW; it does not change the kVA the load draws. If the 32 A figure is a socket rating rather than a measured load, the same sets apply, because a 32 A circuit is normally loaded to no more than 80% continuously.
If the load grows past that, the next step up is a 30 kW (37.5 kVA) unit — see what a 30 kW generator can run for what that size actually powers.
Frequently Asked Questions
What size generator do I need for 32 amps?
A 32 A load needs 7.36 kVA at 230 V single-phase and 22.2 kVA at 400 V three-phase. Add the 125% continuous-duty margin and choose a 10 kVA single-phase set or a 30 kVA three-phase set. A 120 V single-phase 32 A load is only 3.84 kVA, so a 5 kVA set is enough.
How many kW is 32 amps?
32 A is 7.36 kW at 230 V single-phase with a power factor of 1.0, and 5.9 kW at the usual 0.8 power factor. At 400 V three-phase, 32 A is 17.7 kW at 0.8 power factor. The formula is kW = amps × volts × power factor ÷ 1,000.
What is a 32 amp generator?
A generator fitted with a 32 A IEC 60309 industrial socket: the blue 3-pin outlet for 230 V single-phase, common in the UK, Europe and Australia, or the red 5-pin outlet for 400/415 V three-phase. Sets from about 8 kVA upwards carry one. A 10 kVA set delivers about 43 A at 230 V, enough for a 32 A load with margin.
Can an 8 kW generator produce 32 amps?
An 8 kVA set delivers 33.3 A at 240 V; an 8 kW set is normally 10 kVA and delivers 41.7 A. A 32 A load on an 8 kVA set is 96% loading, above the 80% continuous limit, so 10 kVA is the practical choice. Check whether the label quotes peak or continuous kW.
How many kVA do I need for a 32-amp three-phase load?
22.2 kVA at 400 V (1.732 × 400 × 32 ÷ 1,000). At 0.8 power factor that is 17.7 kW of real power. Sized at 125% for continuous duty the set must be 27.7 kVA, so a 30 kVA generator is the standard choice.
What size generator do I need for a 32 amp welder or compressor?
Motors and welders draw two to three times their running current for a few seconds. For direct-on-line starting, size the set at roughly 2–3 times the motor kW or fit a soft starter: a 5.5 kW motor started direct-on-line needs about 15–20 kVA, not the 10 kVA the running load alone suggests.
