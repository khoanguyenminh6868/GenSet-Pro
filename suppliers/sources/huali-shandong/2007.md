Discuss Your 300 kW Requirements
Where This Rating Fits
Compare the required running load with the selected set’s site rating, then check the individual events. Installed nameplate totals describe connected equipment; average demand can conceal a difficult start or sudden removal. A running total below 300 kW alone cannot establish a match. Capacity selection also needs kVA/power factor, phase allocation, starting sequence and confirmed expansion, rather than a fixed spare-capacity percentage.
Review Load Steps and Site Power Conditions
A load step is a change in connected demand. Use the worksheet to describe your own events, starting with what you know. It is an enquiry tool, not a calculated load schedule. Equipment limits and supporting records can follow during technical discussions.

Compare the Start with the Removal
Consider controls and a process drive running when a pump starts. Its acceleration adds demand while the original equipment stays connected. Later, a larger process load disconnects and sensitive controls remain online. Describe the start with its added demand and duration; describe the removal with its size, switching speed and remaining load.
Grouped starts or an automatic retry can create a larger event than the largest single motor. Demand, duration and acceptable voltage/frequency changes matter together. Any revised sequence needs an identified control system, interlock or operator action, with agreed setting and verification responsibilities. Starting permissions, necessary ventilation and protection must remain effective.
Motor, VFD and UPS Input Data
Motor shaft-output kW is not the complete electrical demand. The following data describe what the generator sees:
Direct-on-line (DOL) motors: nameplate, starting-current or locked-rotor data, loaded/unloaded start and acceleration. For a soft starter, add current limit and ramp settings under the driven load.
Variable-frequency drives (VFDs): input demand, power factor, inrush/harmonics, acceleration, filters/reactors and any bypass or recovery mode.
Uninterruptible power supplies (UPS): generator-side rectifier input, battery charging, current-limit and walk-in settings, harmonics and bypass behaviour. Walk-in settings control how input demand increases.
Other rectifier equipment: input characteristics and simultaneous energisation behaviour for the intended operating mode.
UPS output kW alone omits input losses and charging demand. Actual device data help judge the event and the loads that must stay online. There is no universal starting multiplier; fitting a soft starter or VFD does not automatically allow a smaller set.
Ratings and Electrical Configuration
Use the approved data sheet and quotation to establish the supplied configuration, full engine model, Prime/Standby operating conditions, frequency, voltage, phases, current-rating basis and controller/ATS scope. The product name alone cannot establish these details. Engine mechanical output differs from set electrical output because matching includes alternator losses and applicable auxiliary loads. Only at power factor (PF) = 0.8 and on the same rating basis does 300 kW correspond to 375 kVA; the conversion does not prove a nameplate or load-step match. Confirm 50 Hz and 60 Hz separately.
For mixed single-phase and three-phase circuits, check per-phase current, neutral loading, permitted imbalance and large individual loads against the alternator data. Total three-phase output is not available to one single-phase circuit. Transfer equipment, protective devices and cables need the installation design, not just a calculated generator current.
Site Conditions and Available Output
Temperature and altitude affect the engine, alternator and cooling review. For a room or enclosure, use the cooling-air inlet temperature and show inlet/outlet routes, duct restrictions and hot-air recirculation. Outdoor ambient alone may not describe the air reaching the radiator. Exhaust routing and backpressure must meet the selected engine’s limits.
Include indoor/outdoor exposure, maintenance access and acoustic requirements. Establish site output from component data without a universal derating percentage. An enclosure cannot compensate for inadequate ventilation, and an alternator IP rating does not establish whole-set weather protection. Enclosure availability and any fuel, noise, dimensions or weight data belong to the agreed configuration.
Agree the Event and Acceptance Basis
For the important events, agree the following together:
Initial kW/kVA, power factor and connected-equipment state.
Added or removed demand, timing, duration, starter/drive settings and overlaps.
Permitted voltage/frequency changes, recovery criteria, measurement points and methods.
Environment, required records, factory conditions represented and checks needed at site.
A resistive load-bank step does not automatically reproduce motor acceleration or nonlinear input. Production checks, load-bank testing, formal factory acceptance testing (FAT) and site commissioning have different scopes. Agree the available activities, procedure, criteria, records and any witnessing request in the offer. The installed distribution and actual loads may need checks that the factory arrangement cannot represent.
Transfer and Delivery Documents
An automatic transfer switch (ATS) changes the agreed supply path with an interval for detection, starting, stabilisation and switching. An automatic-start controller does not mean an ATS is included. Loads needing continuity require a separately designed solution, including any UPS input matching and autonomy. Synchronising sets and utility-parallel operation are separate scopes.
The offer should define the rated configuration, component identification, electrical/control drawings, operating and maintenance information, and agreed inspection/test records. Project and destination requirements need evidence for the actual equipment and application.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why check a large motor when running power is below 300 kW?
Because starting demand acts while other loads remain connected. Acceleration, grouped starts, retries and the voltage/frequency limits of that equipment determine whether the event is acceptable; steady running power alone cannot settle it.
When does 300 kW correspond to 375 kVA?
At PF = 0.8, with both figures on the same rating and operating basis. Use the approved set data to establish the actual rating; the conversion alone says nothing about motor-starting or load-step suitability.
What information is needed for VFD or UPS loads?
Use generator-side demand, power factor, inrush/harmonics and settings. Include VFD acceleration/bypass and UPS charging, current-limit, walk-in and bypass behaviour. Equipment output power alone does not describe its input demand.
How is output confirmed at high temperature or altitude?
Apply the selected engine, alternator and cooling data to the actual site. Cooling-air inlet temperature, restrictions, recirculation and exhaust backpressure help establish available output and installation limits without a universal derating percentage.
Can 50 Hz and 60 Hz configurations be interchanged?
Not without checking the intended configuration. Confirm speed, alternator, output, voltage and connected-equipment needs for each frequency. A component speed or an alternative-frequency enquiry does not establish the complete set’s rating.
How should load-step verification be agreed?
Specify the initial load, change, timing, settings, environment and measurement method, with voltage/frequency and recovery criteria. Separate what the factory arrangement represents from the checks needing the installed distribution and actual loads.
