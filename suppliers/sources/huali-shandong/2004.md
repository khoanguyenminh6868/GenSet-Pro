Check Whether 100kW Fits Your Load
100kW / 125kVA Generator Overview
For this confirmed prime-power configuration, 100kW corresponds to 125kVA at a power factor of 0.8. A 100kW generator should not be confused with a 100kVA generator, which represents a lower kW output at the same power factor.
How to Check Whether 100kW Is Enough
Running and Simultaneous Loads
Identify the essential loads that may operate at the same time. Total running kW is an important input, but it is not automatically the final generator rating.
Required reserve depends on load profile, motor starting, operating duty, future expansion and project requirements.
Largest Motor and Starting Method
For the largest motor, provide rated power, starting method, starting sequence and the other loads that remain online during starting.
For detailed motor-load selection, see the diesel generator sizing guide for motors, pumps and compressors .
Load Sequence and Future Expansion
Several loads starting together can create a different generator requirement from staged load restoration. Known future loads should also be identified before the capacity is finalized.
80kW vs 100kW vs 160kW Generator Selection
| | Generator Size | When to Evaluate It
| 80kW | When running and starting loads remain within the confirmed capability of the smaller generator.
| 100kW / 125kVA | When the project has higher simultaneous loads, larger motors, more essential equipment or additional capacity requirements.
| 160kW | When running load, starting demand, expansion or site conditions move the requirement beyond the 100kW configuration.
Motors, Pumps and Compressors
Motor-driven loads should not be evaluated only by normal running kW. Pumps, compressors, fans, refrigeration systems, conveyors and production equipment can create higher short-duration demand during starting.
Standby or Prime Duty
The intended operating duty should be stated during quotation. The final rating and operating conditions should follow the confirmed technical specification for the selected configuration.

FAQ About 100kW / 125kVA Generator Selection
Is a 100kW generator the same as a 100kVA generator?
No. At a power factor of 0.8, the confirmed 100kW rating corresponds to 125kVA. A 100kVA generator represents a lower kW output at the same power factor.
When should I choose 100kW instead of 80kW?
Evaluate 100kW when simultaneous load, motor starting, the number of essential loads or future expansion makes the 80kW configuration too limited.
When should I consider a 160kW generator?
A larger generator should be evaluated when running load, starting demand, expansion or site conditions move the project beyond the confirmed capability of the 100kW configuration.
Can a 100kW generator operate motors, pumps or compressors?
Potentially, but final sizing requires the motor rating, starting method, starting current, mechanical load and simultaneous electrical loads.
Is 100kW suitable for factory backup power?
It may be suitable for selected factory loads when the actual load list and starting requirements fit the confirmed generator capability. Factory size alone cannot determine generator capacity.
What information should I send for quotation?
Send required power if known, voltage, frequency, application, operating duty, main loads, largest motor, starting method, installation environment and destination country.
Not sure whether 100kW / 125kVA is enough?
Send your load list, largest motor, starting method, voltage, frequency and application for preliminary configuration review.
