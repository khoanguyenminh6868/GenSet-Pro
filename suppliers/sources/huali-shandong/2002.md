Where This Rating Fits
Separate the total installed load from the equipment needed during an outage. Nonessential sockets and machines may stay disconnected; other loads may be restored later if the process allows. A 30 kW selection depends on simultaneous electrical demand in both kW and kVA, starting performance and site conditions—not the building’s area or the number of appliances.

Why One Motor Can Change the Selection
Consider lighting and controls already operating when a pump starts. The running-load total may look acceptable, but starting the pump adds a different demand. The set must handle that event without exceeding the voltage and frequency variations acceptable to the connected equipment. This illustration does not establish that the 30 kW set can supply a particular pump.
A possible restoration sequence is to identify the connected essential loads, start the pump, then restore equipment that can wait. Actual sequencing depends on the process, controls and protection; some loads cannot be delayed or restarted in that order.
Direct-on-line (DOL): send the motor nameplate, starting-current or locked-rotor data and driven-load condition.
Soft starter: add the current limit, ramp settings and required acceleration.
Variable-frequency drive (VFD): add input data, inrush/harmonic information and any bypass arrangement.
There is no universal starting multiplier. A soft starter or VFD does not automatically make a smaller set suitable.
Ratings and Electrical Configuration
The 30 kW name is a product designation, not proof of a Prime, Standby or Continuous rating. The actual engine model, duty rating, frequency and electrical configuration have not been established in this draft. Before ordering, use a Huali-approved data sheet stating the supplied configuration, electrical output and operating limits. Engine mechanical output must cover alternator losses and applicable auxiliary loads; it is not the set’s electrical output. Assess 50 Hz and 60 Hz configurations separately.
At a rated power factor of 0.8, a confirmed 30 kW electrical rating corresponds to 37.5 kVA on the same rating basis: 30 ÷ 0.8 = 37.5. This relationship does not establish load compatibility or motor-starting performance.
Single-Phase Loads on a Three-Phase Set
In a three-phase 400/230 V system, for example, 230 V phase-to-neutral does not make the entire set output available to one single-phase circuit. This is a system example, not a confirmed voltage for this product. Selection depends on each phase’s current, neutral loading and the alternator’s permitted imbalance.
List large single-phase loads individually and send the phase allocation if available. Redistributing smaller circuits may help balance loading, but an individual large load still needs checking. ATS, breaker and cable selection also requires the system’s protection and installation design.
ATS and the Transfer Interval
An ATS transfers the selected circuits between the normal supply and generator. Failure detection, engine starting, stabilisation and transfer take time. Equipment that cannot tolerate this interval needs a separately designed continuity solution, such as a suitably specified UPS.
An automatic-start controller does not mean an ATS is included. State the required transfer function and agree hardware, interfaces, protection and installation boundaries in the offer. Synchronising sets and utility-parallel operation are separate scopes.
Configuration and Site Requirements
Provide ambient temperature, altitude, indoor/outdoor location, ventilation and exhaust constraints, and maintenance access. These conditions affect equipment selection and available output. Describe any enclosure or acoustic requirement for quotation review; no particular enclosure, dimensions, fuel autonomy or noise value is established here.

When to Compare 50 kW
Compare the 50 kW Weichai diesel generator set if simultaneous demand exceeds the site-rated 30 kW configuration, starting performance is inadequate, site derating reduces available output, or confirmed expansion changes the requirement. Compare the same duty rating, frequency, voltage, phase arrangement and site conditions. A larger total rating does not automatically resolve a large single-phase load; avoid selecting by a fixed spare-capacity percentage.
Frequently Asked Questions
Is 30 kW enough for my essential circuits?
It may be, if running kW and kVA, phase loading and the largest starting event fit the selected set at your site. Start with equipment needed during the outage, then identify what must operate together.
When does 30 kW correspond to 37.5 kVA?
When the confirmed electrical rating is 30 kW at a rated power factor of 0.8 on the same duty basis. The calculation is 30 ÷ 0.8. It does not confirm this product’s actual rating or starting capability.
Can I connect single-phase equipment?
Yes, where the selected alternator and distribution design permit it. Check voltage, per-phase current, neutral loading and imbalance limits. Do not treat the complete three-phase output as available to a single-phase circuit; identify large single-phase loads separately.
What starting details are needed for a pump or compressor?
Send the motor nameplate, starter type, starting-current data and whether the driven equipment starts loaded. Add soft-starter settings or VFD input/bypass details where relevant, plus the loads operating when the motor starts.
Does an ATS guarantee uninterrupted power?
No. Starting and transfer create an interruption. State the outage your equipment can tolerate and identify circuits needing a separate continuity solution. Check ATS inclusion explicitly; an automatic-start controller alone does not establish the transfer equipment supplied.
When should I compare 50 kW?
When simultaneous demand, motor starting, site derating or confirmed expansion exceeds the selected 30 kW configuration. Compare both offers on the same electrical and duty basis. A higher total rating still requires a separate check of phase loading.
