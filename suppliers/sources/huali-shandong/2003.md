Where This Rating Fits
Separate the equipment installed on site from the loads that need generator power, then identify what must run together. A workshop may need intermittent machines assessed against its base load; a farm may need overlapping ventilation and pumping; refrigeration auxiliary circuits need their own scope. These are selection situations, not verified supply packages. A running total below 50 kW still leaves motor starting to be checked.

Assess the Starting Event, Not Just the Largest Nameplate
Consider controls and ventilation already running when a pump starts. Although the running total may look acceptable, the pump adds a starting demand while those base loads remain connected. This is an illustration, not a customer case or proof of capacity. The largest event may instead be several smaller motors starting together, or an automatic retry overlapping another start. Demand, duration, existing load and acceptable voltage/frequency changes all matter; one starting-kVA threshold cannot settle the selection.
Direct-on-line (DOL): motor nameplate, starting-current or locked-rotor data, and loaded or unloaded starting condition.
Soft starter: current limit, ramp settings and required acceleration under the driven load.
Variable-frequency drive (VFD): input demand, inrush/harmonic data and any bypass arrangement.
These details show how the motor and drive load the set during acceleration. There is no universal starting multiplier; a soft starter or VFD does not automatically permit a smaller generator.
Which Loads Restart After Transfer to the Generator?
Normal operating overlap can differ from restart overlap. Once the generator takes the load, contactors, fan groups, pumps and refrigeration equipment may all seek to restart, even though operators normally start them separately. Check starting permissives, automatic retries and the permitted order with the equipment controls. This concerns restoration on generator power, rather than transfer back to the utility.
Sequencing can change the event only where the process and protection allow it. Do not apply a common delay to every load, postpone essential ventilation without assessment, or bypass restart protection. The actual control sequence is part of the load assessment.
Ratings and Electrical Configuration
The 50 kW designation does not establish a Prime, Standby or Continuous rating. The Huali-approved data sheet for the supplied set must identify the full engine model, electrical output, duty, frequency, voltage, phases and site limits. Engine mechanical output must cover alternator losses and applicable auxiliary loads; it is not the set’s electrical output. Assess 50 Hz and 60 Hz configurations separately.
Only at PF = 0.8 and on the same rating basis does 50 kW ÷ 0.8 correspond to 62.5 kVA. That relationship alone does not establish the supplied rating or starting capability.
Mixing Single-Phase and Three-Phase Equipment
A three-phase 400/230 V system is an example, not a confirmed specification for this set: 400 V is line-to-line and 230 V is phase-to-neutral. Its full three-phase output cannot be assigned to one single-phase circuit. A phase schedule should show each load’s voltage and current, including substantial single-phase loads. Check per-phase current, neutral loading and permitted imbalance against the selected alternator. Distribution, ATS, breaker and cable selection also require the installation’s protection design.
Open or Sound-Attenuated: Define the Site Requirement
Describe the installation location, ambient temperature, altitude, air inlet/outlet paths, exhaust route and maintenance space. For an open arrangement, identify the room or shelter and weather protection needed. For a sound-attenuated arrangement, add the acoustic target and nearby sensitive locations. These requirements guide the quotation; they do not confirm enclosure availability. An enclosure cannot compensate for inadequate ventilation, and alternator IP21 does not establish whole-set weather protection.
Fuel consumption: identify the engine or complete set, load basis, frequency and operating conditions. Engine g/kWh is not automatically set L/h or a runtime guarantee.
Noise: identify dB(A), measurement distance, load, enclosure/structure and measurement conditions. Compare data on equivalent bases.
ATS, Factory Checks and Documents
An ATS transfers the agreed circuits between supplies, with an interval for detection, starting, stabilisation and transfer. Automatic engine starting does not mean an ATS is included. Confirm transfer equipment, interfaces and protection in the offer. Loads requiring continuity need separate design. Synchronising sets and utility-parallel operation are separate control and protection scopes.
Agree the available documentation and acceptance scope in the quotation:
Set/component identification, rated data and required electrical/control drawings.
Operating/maintenance information and the inspection or test records to be delivered.
Any project-specific FAT procedure, acceptance criteria, records and witnessing arrangements.
Routine/production checks, load-bank testing, formal factory acceptance testing (FAT) and site commissioning are distinct activities. The agreed scope determines what is performed and documented; installed transfer, distribution and restart behaviour also need verification.

When to Compare 30 kW or 100 kW
Consider the 30 kW Weichai diesel generator set for a smaller essential-load scope that fits its site rating and starting capability. Compare the 100 kW Weichai diesel generator set when assessed running demand, starting events, site derating or confirmed expansion exceeds the selected 50 kW set.
First identify whether the constraint is grouped restarting, phase allocation or control logic. Increasing kW alone may not resolve it. Compare the same duty rating, frequency, voltage, phases and environment, including actual alternator performance; avoid a fixed spare-capacity percentage.
Frequently Asked Questions
Can 50 kW handle my mixed loads?
It may, if running demand, phase loading and starting events fit the selected set at the site. Include the base load present during motor starts and the loads that restart together, rather than checking only total running kW.
How can I check whether the largest motor will start?
Use the motor and starter data with the connected base load, acceleration time and acceptable voltage/frequency changes. Also check grouped starts and retries: the largest individual motor may not create the largest event.
What if several fans or refrigeration loads restart together?
Assess their actual post-transfer sequence, starting permissives and retries as a group. Staggering may help where equipment and process requirements permit it; essential ventilation and protection requirements can restrict delays.
How should single-phase and three-phase loads be distributed?
Allocate circuits using a phase schedule, then check each phase, the neutral and the alternator’s imbalance limits. Large single-phase loads need individual assessment; the total three-phase rating is not available to one circuit.
How should fuel consumption and noise figures be confirmed?
Use data tied to the quoted equipment and conditions. Consumption needs its load basis and frequency; noise needs dB(A), distance, load, structure and measurement conditions. Isolated figures are insufficient for comparison.
When should I compare 30 kW or 100 kW?
Compare 30 kW for a smaller assessed scope, or 100 kW when demand exceeds the selected 50 kW configuration. Check phase limits and restart logic first; a larger rating does not automatically solve either problem.
