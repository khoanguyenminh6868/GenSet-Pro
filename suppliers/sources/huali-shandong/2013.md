Is a Single 1500kW Generator the Right Architecture?
A single 1500kW unit can be appropriate, but the power rating alone does not determine the best architecture.
A project with one HL1500GF has a different operating and maintenance strategy from a plant using two or more generators in parallel. The choice becomes important where the facility has strict availability targets, future expansion plans, large variations in operating load or limited opportunities to shut down the generating system for maintenance.
Start by defining the required operating case. Identify what must remain powered if the normal source fails, what can be shed, and whether the entire 1500kW-class capacity must be available from one generating source.
A single generator can simplify the number of generator breakers, synchronizing devices and controls. A multi-generator system can provide other operating possibilities, but it also adds switchgear, protection, communications and control logic.
Neither architecture is automatically better.
For a meaningful comparison, provide the load schedule, redundancy target, expected future load, maintenance requirements and single-line diagram where available.
Comparing Lower-Power Generator Options
If the verified load, motor-starting demand and reserve requirement are below the 1500kW range, the 1200kW Perkins Diesel Generator can be reviewed as the adjacent lower-output Perkins reference.
If the required power is lower and the project is also open to another engine platform, the 1000kW Cummins Diesel Generator is a separate lower-output reference. It is not equivalent to the HL1500GF and requires its own load, voltage and configuration review.
Project Load, Motor Starting and Load Steps
A connected-load total does not show how the generator will actually be loaded.
For generator sizing, distinguish between:
installed load;
normally operating load;
maximum credible simultaneous load;
loads that can be delayed or shed;
future loads already planned.
This distinction becomes particularly important when several large motors, pumps, compressors or process loads are involved.
Largest Motor
Generator kW alone cannot establish whether the HL1500GF can start a particular large motor.
For each major motor, provide:
motor kW or HP;
rated voltage;
starting method;
starting-current information where available;
mechanical load during starting;
loads already connected when the motor starts.
A direct-on-line motor, a soft-started motor and a VFD-driven motor can impose very different transient demands on the generating system.
Load Restoration and Future Capacity
The sequence after generator startup is also important.
Restoring a large portion of plant load in one step produces a different transient condition from introducing essential loads in several controlled stages. For a standby plant, define which load returns first, which major motors start later and whether non-critical loads can wait.
Future expansion should be treated in the same way. Add capacity for a defined expansion plan rather than simply applying an arbitrary percentage to the current running load.
Prime vs Standby Duty
The HL1500GF confirmed ratings are:
Prime: 1500 kW / 1875 kVA
Standby: 1650 kW / 2062.5 kVA
These ratings represent different operating duties and should not be treated as interchangeable.
For a prime-power project, provide the expected annual operating pattern, normal load range and significant load changes.
For a standby installation, define the normal source, transfer arrangement, critical loads and restoration sequence after loss of utility power.
Prime power should not be presented as an undefined continuous-power rating. If a project requires another operating duty, that requirement needs to be identified before the generator configuration is finalized.
Perkins 4016TAG2A and Huali HL1500GF Configuration
The confirmed HL1500GF uses a Perkins 4016TAG2A diesel engine at 1500 rpm for the 50 Hz configuration.
The engine has a 16-cylinder V arrangement, 61.123 L displacement, 160 × 190 mm bore and stroke, and 13.6:1 compression ratio. Its intake system is turbocharged and air-to-air charge cooled. The engine uses direct injection, liquid cooling, a 24 V starting system and electronic governing.
These are engine characteristics. They should not be confused with the complete generator-set electrical output.
Huali integrates the Perkins engine with the Shanhua HLF1500 alternator , baseframe, cooling arrangement, controls, electrical connection and other equipment defined by the order.
For the confirmed alternator configuration, HLF1500 and IP21 protection with Class H insulation can be identified on the product page. AVR model, standalone alternator rated output and excitation details should only be added when they are confirmed for the actual HLF1500 supplied.
What Does 2706A Mean for the Electrical System?
The confirmed rated current of the 1875kVA, 400V HL1500GF is 2706 A.
For a three-phase system:
I ≈ kVA × 1000 / (&radic;3 × V)
Using the confirmed prime rating:
I ≈ 1875 × 1000 / (&radic;3 × 400) ≈ 2706 A
This close agreement provides a useful consistency check between the approved kVA rating, voltage and rated current.
The engineering consequence is more important than the formula itself.
At approximately 2.7 kA, the project team must account for the physical and electrical interface between the generator and the distribution system. Generator terminals, breaker arrangement, busbar or parallel cable routing, switchboard termination space and access for installation become significant design items.
The page should not prescribe one breaker frame, cable size or busbar section for all 1500kW projects. Those selections require the approved equipment data, installation method, fault level, protection study and applicable electrical standards.
Breaker, Busbar and Switchgear Interface
For a 400V HL1500GF project, electrical routing should be discussed early rather than after the generator room and switchboard positions have already been fixed.
Useful inputs include:
generator-to-switchboard distance;
existing or proposed switchboard location;
cable or busbar preference;
conductor material where already specified;
termination-space limitations;
project fault level;
required protection and interlocking philosophy.
A 2706A interface may involve substantial busbar or multiple-conductor arrangements. The actual solution belongs to the project electrical design.
This is also why physical layout matters. A design that satisfies electrical ampacity but provides inadequate bending radius, termination access or maintenance clearance can still create installation problems.
Single Generator, Parallel System or Transformer/MV Interface?
1500kW does not automatically mean that the project should use parallel generators or medium-voltage generation.
A single HL1500GF may be appropriate where one unit satisfies capacity, availability and maintenance requirements.
Parallel generators become worth evaluating where the project needs defined redundancy, staged capacity, future expansion or the ability to keep part of the generating plant available while another unit is maintained.
A parallel arrangement introduces additional requirements, including synchronizing, generator breakers, protection, load sharing, communications and operating logic.
High current on the 400V side can also lead project engineers to compare direct low-voltage distribution with a transformer-connected or medium-voltage architecture.
That comparison should be driven by the complete electrical system, including distribution distance, existing plant voltage, transformer arrangement, switchgear, fault level and local design requirements.
A 1500kW rating by itself is not a reason to specify medium voltage.
When these alternatives are under review, provide the single-line diagram and proposed electrical architecture before the generator and switchgear scope is finalized.
Synchronizing, Protection and Control Scope
The required control system follows the operating architecture.
A utility-to-generator standby system has different requirements from generator-to-generator paralleling or a multiple-source installation involving generators, transformers and other incoming supplies.
Where synchronizing is required, define the sources that can operate in parallel, breaker interfaces, operating sequence, transfer philosophy and required protection functions.
ATS should also be treated according to the system architecture. A transfer switch may be part of a straightforward standby system, but it does not replace complete switchgear engineering where multiple high-current sources and protection interfaces are involved.
Controller brand, controller model, synchronizing functions and remote monitoring should only be described as supplied features after they are included in the approved Huali project scope.
Cooling, Ventilation, Exhaust and Fuel
A high-power generator room must move sufficient cooling air without allowing hot radiator discharge air to recirculate into the intake.
For an indoor HL1500GF project, provide the maximum ambient temperature, altitude and proposed room arrangement. The installation review should address fresh-air intake, radiator discharge, duct restrictions and exhaust routing using the approved project data.
Do not copy airflow requirements, exhaust back-pressure limits or room dimensions from another generator model.
Fuel-system planning begins with required runtime.
A standby project designed for a defined emergency duration can have a different day-tank and bulk-storage arrangement from a prime-power installation operating for long periods.
Provide the required fuel autonomy and any limitations on fuel storage, transfer or refilling. Tank sizing should use the applicable fuel-consumption data at specified operating loads rather than a single generic consumption figure.
Room, Container, Foundation and Maintenance Access
The HL1500GF may be considered in an open configuration for a properly engineered generator room, or in another enclosure arrangement where the confirmed project scope supports it.
Projects requiring outdoor weather protection or defined acoustic treatment can review a silent diesel generator configuration according to the required acoustic, weather-protection and maintenance conditions.
For packaged outdoor projects, a containerized diesel generator configuration can also be evaluated where the project benefits from an integrated enclosure, internal service space or packaged auxiliary equipment.
The enclosure name alone does not establish a guaranteed noise level.
Foundation design, equipment weight, lifting points and vibration treatment require the approved general arrangement and project data. Complete genset dimensions or weight must not be taken from the Perkins engine dimensions.
Before production, verify the route from the delivery point to the final foundation, including road access, gates, building openings and crane position.
Maintenance planning should consider more than routine filter access. Leave sufficient space for work on the engine, alternator, cooling system, electrical terminals and control equipment and for removal of major components when necessary.
FAT, Documentation and Commissioning Scope
At this power level, the acceptance package should be defined early enough to affect production and testing if required.
Depending on the agreed project scope, the buyer or EPC team may request component verification, electrical checks, controller and protection checks, operating tests, load-bank testing or witnessed FAT.
Witness points and acceptance criteria are most useful when agreed before the factory test rather than introduced after the equipment is complete.
Project documentation may include, where included in the order scope:
approved technical datasheet;
general arrangement drawing;
electrical drawings;
component information;
factory test records;
project-specific inspection or shipping documents.
Commissioning responsibility must also be clear. Factory testing, site installation, site commissioning and final system acceptance are different stages and should not be treated as the same service unless the contract specifically includes them.

FAQ About the 1500kW Perkins Generator
Is 1500kW the same as 1875kVA?
At a power factor of 0.8, 1875kVA corresponds to 1500kW. The confirmed HL1500GF prime rating is 1500 kW / 1875 kVA.
How much current does an 1875kVA generator produce at 400V?
The confirmed HL1500GF rated current is 2706 A . The three-phase calculation for 1875kVA at 400V gives approximately the same value. This high current must be considered when planning breakers, busbars, cables, switchgear and termination space.
Can the HL1500GF start a large motor?
The 1500kW rating alone cannot answer that question. Motor kW or HP, voltage, starting method, starting current, mechanical load and the loads already connected at the time of starting are required for a meaningful review.
Is one 1500kW generator better than several generators in parallel?
No universal answer applies. A single unit can simplify the generating plant, while parallel generators may support redundancy, staged capacity, expansion or maintenance objectives. The load profile and required availability determine which architecture deserves further evaluation.
Does a 1500kW generator need medium voltage?
No. The confirmed HL1500GF reference configuration is 400/230V. A transformer-connected or MV architecture becomes a project-level question when current, distribution distance, existing electrical infrastructure or switchgear design makes it relevant.
What should I send for a 1500kW generator quotation?
Send the load schedule, voltage and frequency, prime or standby duty, largest motors, starting methods, load sequence, redundancy requirement, installation conditions and destination country. Include the single-line diagram for projects involving parallel generators, transformers or complex switchgear.
