Discuss Your 200kW Generator Requirement
Running Load and Starting Demand Are Different
A generator has to do two different jobs when motor-driven equipment is involved. First, it must carry the normal operating load after the equipment reaches its working condition. Second, it must support the transient electrical and mechanical demand while a motor accelerates.
A project may therefore have a simultaneous running load that appears comfortably below the generator rating while still requiring a separate review of one large pump, compressor, fan, conveyor or production motor.
The critical question is not only how many kilowatts are running, but also what the most demanding starting event is and what other loads are already connected when it occurs.
Information Needed to Assess Motor Starting
| | Equipment | Starting Method | Starting Current / Time or Manufacturer Data | Loads Already Online During Start | Allowable Variation
| Large electric motor | DOL / star-delta / soft starter / VFD / other | Starting current, locked-rotor data, acceleration time or motor/starter data | State the running loads already connected | Voltage and frequency limits, if known
| Pump | State motor starter type | Motor and pump data, starting current where available | Other pumps, controls, lighting and process loads | Acceptable voltage/frequency variation and process limits
| Air or refrigeration compressor | State starter or drive type | Motor data, controller data and starting duration | Other compressors, dryers, fans, pumps and controls | Equipment and controller limits
| Production machine | State actual starting arrangement | Manufacturer electrical data where available | Process equipment already online | Machine or control-system tolerance
| Future major motor load | Proposed starting method | Preliminary manufacturer information | Planned future operating scenario | Project design requirement
This table is an information checklist, not a generator starting-capability table. It does not state that the 200 kW set can start any particular motor size.
Why the Largest Motor Rating Alone Is Not Enough
A motor nameplate is an important starting point, but its rated kW alone does not establish the generator requirement. The review may also need rated voltage and phase, full-load current, starting or locked-rotor current where available, starter or drive type, acceleration settings, driven equipment condition during startup, other loads already online and acceptable voltage and frequency variation.
A motor starting first on a lightly loaded generator creates a different event from the same motor starting after several pumps, controls, lighting circuits and production loads are already operating.
For a broader technical guide, see our diesel generator sizing guide for motors, pumps and compressors .
Direct-on-Line Starting
A direct-on-line motor is connected directly to the supply during starting. For generator selection, useful information includes the actual motor data, starting-current information, mechanical load during acceleration and the other loads already operating.
Do not apply one fixed DOL multiplier to every project. Motor design, supply conditions, acceleration time and driven equipment can change the result.
Soft Starter
A soft starter can reduce the electrical and mechanical impact of acceleration, but the words “soft starter” do not remove the need for a starting assessment.
Provide the motor rating, soft-starter model and rating, current-limit setting where known, ramp time, bypass arrangement, driven load condition and loads already online.
A current-limited start may last longer than a direct start, so current magnitude alone is not the full assessment.
Variable Frequency Drive
A VFD can provide controlled acceleration and may reduce the direct motor inrush seen at the supply. The generator review should still consider the VFD input rating, acceleration settings, driven load, input characteristics where relevant, other nonlinear loads, generator voltage and frequency requirements and control-system compatibility.
A VFD should not be treated as automatic proof that generator capacity is sufficient.
Pump Starting Conditions
Pump motor power is only the first item to check. The project should clarify the pump motor rating, starting method, duty/standby arrangement, number of pumps that may run together, whether pumps can start simultaneously, the starting hydraulic condition and other loads already online.
Two projects using the same motor may create different generator requirements if their pump systems start under different hydraulic conditions.
Compressor Starting Conditions
Compressor startup needs particular attention because the mechanical condition during restart can materially affect the event.
Useful information includes compressor type, motor rating, starter or VFD, loaded or unloaded start, unload-control arrangement, system pressure before restart, restart logic, cooling fans, dryers, pumps and control-system loads, and other equipment already online.
A compressor designed to start unloaded should not automatically be assessed as though it always starts loaded. Likewise, a system should not be assumed to start unloaded without verifying its control logic.
Voltage and Frequency Tolerance Matter
A successful motor start is not defined only by whether the motor eventually accelerates. Other connected equipment may have limits for voltage dip, frequency deviation, contactor hold-in voltage, PLC or control-power stability, VFD or soft-starter input and process interruption.
For sensitive production equipment, provide the manufacturer's permissible electrical limits where available. The allowable transient response should be agreed against the actual equipment and proposed generator configuration rather than using one universal limit.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why is total running kW not enough for selection?
Motor-driven loads have different electrical conditions during starting and normal operation. A generator may carry the stable running load but still require additional transient capability when the largest motor, pump or compressor starts while other equipment is already online.
What motor-starting data should I provide?
Start with the motor nameplate, voltage, rated current, starting method and any available starting-current or manufacturer data. Also state the driven equipment, whether it starts loaded, and what other loads are already operating.
Does a soft starter remove the need for assessment?
No. A soft starter may reduce starting current, but its effect depends on current-limit settings, ramp time, bypass arrangement, motor acceleration and mechanical load. The actual settings should be reviewed.
Does compressor loading affect startup?
Yes. Loaded and unloaded starting conditions can create different mechanical and electrical demands. Compressor type, unloading logic, remaining system pressure and auxiliary loads should be identified where possible.
How does 200kW relate to 250kVA?
When the approved generator electrical rating is 200 kW and the approved power factor is 0.8, the corresponding apparent power is 250 kVA because kVA = kW / PF. This calculation does not establish the rating basis by itself; the approved datasheet and nameplate remain the controlling documents.
When should a different rating or configuration be reviewed?
Review another rating or configuration when the confirmed running load, most demanding starting event, electrical tolerance, site conditions or documented future load show that the proposed 200 kW configuration does not match the operating case.
