Diesel Generator Powered by DCEC Cummins (18–480 kW)
Description
Each set is built around B, C, L and Q series diesel engines from DCEC. DCEC makes engines, not complete gensets — the alternator, control panel, skid and canopy are matched to the engine and confirmed on the order. Alternators are Stamford, Marathon or Leroy Somer etc.
DCEC states a power range of 15 kW to 450 kW from a plant of 26.23 hectares with more than 1,900 employees, an annual engine capacity of 160,000 units and registered capital above USD 100 million.
The tables below are what we actually build: 32 models at 50 Hz from 18 to 440 kW, and 24 models at 60 Hz from 22 to 480 kW.
Above 480 kW there is no DCEC option. The CCEC range covers 220 to 1,650 kW on the same Cummins engine platform.
Model numbers ending C1 are 50 Hz, C2 are 60 Hz.
Features
One rating, several engines. WDL-88C1 appears several times in the 50 Hz table below. Those are not duplicate rows. The genset nameplate reads the same; the engine underneath does not.
Quote both model numbers. Every row carries a genset model and an engine model, because the engine model is what gets built. 6BT5.9-G1 and 6BT5.9-G2 share a block but not a rating.
Global warranty, because the engine is genuine. Cummins factory warranty follows the engine serial number, not the invoice. Any authorized Cummins service point, in any country, can look up that serial and honour the claim. That is the whole point of buying a factory-new engine instead of a rebuilt one — and it is a promise a grey-market seller cannot make, because the serial would give them away.
Every engine is new, and you can prove it. The engine carries a factory plate and a QR code. Scan it, match the serial against the number on your order confirmation, and the unit is confirmed as factory-new. No rebuilt units.
We keep the serial on file. Every unit we ship is logged by engine serial number. A warranty question three years later can be traced to the build and the delivery date, without your customer digging through paperwork.
