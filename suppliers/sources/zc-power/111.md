Power Fundamentals
Understanding Generator Power Output: kW vs kVA
Generator power output is measured in two critical units: kilowatts (kW) and kilovolt-amperes (kVA). Understanding the difference prevents the costly mistake of undersizing or oversizing your power solution.
kW (Kilowatts) represents the real power your generator delivers to operate equipment. This is the actual working power that performs useful work—running motors, lighting, heating, and computing equipment.
kVA (Kilovolt-Amperes) represents apparent power—the total power the generator must produce, including both real power (kW) and reactive power required by inductive loads like motors and transformers.
The relationship between kW and kVA is defined by power factor , typically 0.8 for most industrial applications. This means a 100kVA generator delivers approximately 80kW of real power. Industrial facilities with heavy motor loads may experience lower power factors, requiring larger kVA ratings to deliver the same kW output.

Sizing Methodology
How to Calculate Your Generator Power Requirements
Accurate generator sizing requires understanding your load profile, including running watts, starting surge requirements, and power factor. Follow this methodology to determine your required power output range.
Step 1: Inventory All Loads
List every electrical device the generator will power, including motors, lighting, HVAC, and specialized equipment. Record both running wattage and starting wattage—motor starting currents can be 3-6 times running current.
Step 2: Apply Diversity Factor
Not all equipment operates simultaneously. Apply appropriate diversity factors (typically 0.7-0.9 for commercial applications) to avoid oversizing.
Step 3: Account for Power Factor
Divide total kW requirements by power factor (typically 0.8) to determine required kVA rating. A facility requiring 400kW with 0.8 power factor needs a 500kVA generator minimum.
Step 4: Include Safety Margin
Add 20-25% capacity margin for future expansion and optimal loading. Generators operate most efficiently at 70-80% of rated capacity.
Step 5: Consider Environmental Derating
High altitude and elevated temperatures reduce generator output. At 3,000m elevation, a generator produces approximately 88% of sea-level rated output—factor this into your sizing calculations.

Environmental Considerations
Environmental Factors: Altitude and Temperature Derating
Generator power output decreases with increasing altitude and ambient temperature. Engine combustion efficiency drops in thin air, while cooling system capacity reduces at high temperatures. Understanding these derating factors ensures your generator delivers required output in actual operating conditions.
Altitude Derating: Diesel engines lose approximately 4% power output for every 1,000 meters (3,300 feet) above sea level. A 500kVA generator rated at sea level produces approximately 420kVA at 4,000m elevation.
Temperature Derating: Ambient temperatures above 40°C (104°F) reduce cooling efficiency, typically requiring 2-3% output reduction per 5°C above rating.

Generator Power Output Range FAQs
What size generator do I need for my facility?
Generator sizing depends on total electrical load, motor starting requirements, and power factor. Calculate running watts for all equipment, apply starting surge multipliers (3-6x for motors), convert to kVA using power factor (typically 0.8), and add 20-25% safety margin. ZC Power provides free engineering consultation to ensure accurate sizing for your specific application.
What is the difference between kW and kVA?
kW (kilowatts) measures real power that performs useful work. kVA (kilovolt-amperes) measures apparent power including both real power and reactive power required by inductive loads. The relationship is: kVA = kW ÷ Power Factor. Most industrial generators are rated at 0.8 power factor, meaning a 100kVA generator delivers 80kW of real power.
How does altitude affect generator power output?
Diesel generators lose approximately 4% output capacity for every 1,000 meters (3,300 feet) above sea level due to reduced air density affecting combustion. At 3,000m elevation, a generator produces roughly 88% of its sea-level rating. ZC Power offers high-altitude packages with enhanced turbocharging and cooling to maintain rated output at elevation.

Can I parallel generators for more power?
Yes. ZC Power generators from 200kVA upward support parallel synchronization, allowing multiple units to operate together as a single power source. This provides scalable capacity (two 1,000kVA units = 2,000kVA total), redundancy (if one unit requires maintenance, others continue operating), and improved load management. Parallel systems require synchronization panels and compatible control systems.
What is the optimal load range for a generator?
Generators operate most efficiently at 70-80% of rated capacity. Continuous under-loading (below 30%) causes wet stacking, carbon buildup, and premature engine wear. Over-loading (above 90% for extended periods) causes overheating and reduced lifespan. Size your generator so normal operating load falls within the 70-80% range, with capacity for occasional peak demands.
Which engine brand is best for my power requirements?
Cummins offers excellent data center reliability and global parts availability. Perkins provides fuel efficiency and quieter operation ideal for hospitals and offices. Yuchai and Weichai deliver cost-effective performance for budget-conscious projects. Baudouin specializes in marine and heavy industrial applications. ZC Power engineers recommend the optimal engine based on your duty cycle, environmental conditions, and support requirements.
