Technical Excellence
The Hidden Risk: Wet Stacking and Why Load Bank Testing Matters
The most common—and most preventable—cause of premature 1001+ kW generator failure is underloading. When diesel generators operate below 30% of rated capacity, unburned fuel and carbon deposits accumulate in the exhaust system, a condition called “wet stacking.” Over time, this leads to:
Carbon buildup on valves and behind piston rings
Cylinder liner polishing and loss of compression
Reduced fuel efficiency and power output
The ZC Power Solution:
Every 1001+ kW generator set leaving our facility undergoes comprehensive load bank testing at 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of rated load. We provide full documentation of voltage stability, frequency regulation, and temperature performance.

1001+ kW Generator: Frequently Asked Questions
How much does a 1001 kW generator cost?
Pricing ranges from $90,000 for mid-range Tier 2/3 units to $570,000 for premium Tier 4 Final Cummins or Caterpillar generators with full enclosures. Refurbished low-hour units offer exceptional value at $140,000-$200,000. Complete project costs including ATS, fuel tank, and installation add $15,000-$50,000. ZC Power provides factory-direct pricing without middleman markups.
What can a 1000 kW generator actually power?
A 1000 kW (1250 kVA) generator can power a 150-room hospital including emergency rooms and operating theaters, a 50,000+ square foot cold storage facility, a 200-rack data center with cooling, or a multi-line manufacturing plant. Proper sizing requires calculating both steady-state loads and motor starting (LRA) surge requirements, which we provide free with every inquiry.
How much fuel does a 1000 kW generator use per hour?
Fuel consumption varies by load: approximately 70-75 gallons per hour at 100% load, 52-55 GPH at 75% load, and 38-40 GPH at 50% load. For NFPA 110 Class 48 (48 hours runtime), you need 3,600+ gallons of on-site diesel storage. Natural gas generators eliminate fuel storage but depend on utility gas supply availability during grid outages.
What is wet stacking and how do I prevent it?
Wet stacking occurs when diesel generators operate below 30% load, causing unburned fuel and carbon deposits to accumulate in the exhaust system. Prevent it through annual 2-4 hour load bank testing at 75-100% rated load to burn off deposits. All ZC Power 1001+ kW units ship with load bank testing documentation, and we offer ongoing testing services to maintain peak performance.
What’s the lead time for a 1000 kW generator?
Traditional OEM lead times for new Tier 4 units currently exceed 50-70 weeks. ZC Power maintains in-stock inventory of 1001-2000 kW generators with 14-21 day delivery to most global destinations. For custom configurations, factory production typically requires 4-6 weeks—still significantly faster than OEM channels.
What emissions tier do I need for my location?
Emergency standby applications in most U.S. states (including Texas and Florida) and international markets allow Tier 2 or Tier 3 generators, avoiding the complexity and maintenance overhead of Tier 4 Final SCR/DPF systems. Prime power or non-emergency applications in EPA-regulated areas typically require Tier 4 Final. Our engineering team verifies emissions requirements for your specific jurisdiction.
