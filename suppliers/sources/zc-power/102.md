Fuel Selection
Diesel vs Natural Gas: Engineering the Right Choice
Diesel generators remain the industry standard for mission-critical standby applications. Their advantages include superior energy density (129 BTU per unit versus 37 for natural gas), faster load acceptance (100% rated load in 10 seconds), and operational independence from utility infrastructure. Diesel engines typically deliver 20-30 year service lifespans with proper maintenance. For facilities requiring blackout-proof reliability—hospitals, data centers, emergency services—diesel is the conservative, proven choice.
Natural gas generators excel in prime power and continuous operation scenarios. With fuel costs 30-70% lower than diesel and cleaner emissions profiles (30% less CO2, significantly lower NOx and particulates), natural gas becomes economically advantageous after approximately 800-2,000 annual operating hours. The elimination of on-site fuel storage simplifies permitting and reduces environmental compliance burdens.

New vs Used: Making the Smart Investment Decision
The decision between new and used 101-300 kW generators involves balancing capital constraints against risk tolerance, warranty requirements, and operational criticality. Understanding the trade-offs ensures you allocate budget appropriately for your reliability requirements.
New generator sets deliver maximum reliability assurance with full manufacturer warranties, zero operating hours, and the latest emissions compliance. For healthcare facilities, data centers, and other applications where downtime is measured in lives lost or millions in revenue, new equipment is the prudent choice. Lead times typically range 8-16 weeks for standard configurations, with expedited manufacturing available for urgent requirements.
Certified pre-owned generators offer substantial capital savings—typically 40-60% below new equipment—while maintaining acceptable reliability for less critical applications. We subject every used unit to our 31-point inspection protocol including load bank testing, compression analysis, and thermal imaging. Critical caveat: Only consider used generators with verifiable hour meters, complete service records, and available engine manufacturer support.
Our inventory includes both new factory-direct units and certified pre-owned generators from leading manufacturers. We provide honest assessments of which configuration suits your application rather than pushing higher-margin new equipment.

What size building will a 300 kW generator power?
A 300 kW generator reliably supports 25,000 to 50,000 square feet depending on application density. General commercial buildings (offices, warehouses) typically achieve 50,000 sq ft coverage. High-density applications (hospitals, data centers, manufacturing) require more power per square foot, reducing coverage to approximately 25,000 sq ft. Always conduct a professional load analysis for accurate sizing.
How much fuel does a 300 kW diesel generator use per hour?
At 75% load, a 300 kW diesel generator consumes approximately 21 gallons (79 liters) per hour. At full rated load, consumption increases to roughly 25-27 gallons per hour. Fuel consumption scales proportionally with load—a 50% load reduces consumption to approximately 14 gallons per hour. These figures assume modern engines meeting Tier 3 emissions standards.
Diesel vs natural gas: which is better for commercial use?
Choose diesel for standby emergency power under 500 annual operating hours, especially for mission-critical applications requiring blackout-proof reliability. Select natural gas for prime power exceeding 1,000 hours annually, where lower fuel costs and cleaner emissions justify higher initial investment. Our engineers analyze your specific load profile, runtime requirements, and regulatory environment to recommend the optimal fuel type.
How long do 300 kW generators last?
With proper maintenance, 300 kW diesel generators typically deliver 20-30 year service lifespans (10,000-30,000 operating hours). Natural gas units generally achieve 10-15 years under heavy continuous use. Standby applications where generators exercise weekly but rarely carry load often achieve the longest service lives. Major overhauls at 15,000-20,000 hours can extend diesel generator life by another decade.
What maintenance does a 300 kW generator require?
Routine maintenance includes oil and filter changes every 250 hours or 6 months, coolant system checks, battery inspection and charging system verification, and fuel system monitoring. Annual maintenance costs range $3,000-$7,000 for standby applications and $15,000-$25,000 for prime power. We recommend preventive maintenance contracts to ensure 94% reliability versus 67% for minimally maintained units.
