Why Three-Phase Generators Are Essential for Industrial Operations
Three-phase generators deliver power through three alternating currents, each offset by 120 degrees. This continuous power flow eliminates the voltage dips inherent in single-phase systems, providing the stable, high-capacity electricity that industrial motors, HVAC systems, and heavy machinery demand.
For operations requiring more than 40 kW of power, three-phase systems offer distinct advantages: 92-94% efficiency ratings compared to 85-88% for single-phase, balanced torque that extends equipment lifespan by 30-40%, and superior motor starting capability for heavy loads.
Whether you need standby power for emergency backup or prime power for continuous operation, selecting the right three-phase generator set is a critical infrastructure decision.

Sizing Guide
How to Size Your Three-Phase Generator Correctly
Proper sizing is the most critical factor in generator selection. An undersized unit will fail under load, while an oversized unit operates inefficiently and risks ‘wet stacking’—unburned fuel accumulation that damages diesel engines.
Step 1: Calculate Total Running Load
Sum the wattage of all equipment that must operate simultaneously. Include motors, HVAC systems, lighting, and essential production equipment. Remember that three-phase systems typically have a 0.8 power factor—your actual power output is 80% of the kVA rating.
Step 2: Account for Starting Current
Electric motors draw up to 200% of their running current during startup. If your facility has multiple large motors, consider a load management system that staggers startup sequences, allowing a smaller generator to handle larger total loads.
Step 3: Add 20-30% Headroom
Cummins and other major manufacturers recommend maintaining minimum 30% load to prevent wet stacking. Factor in future expansion and load fluctuations to ensure long-term reliability.
Voltage Options
Understanding Three-Phase Voltage Options
Selecting the correct voltage is as important as selecting the right capacity. Your generator must match your facility’s distribution system and equipment requirements.
208V (120/208V)
Common in light commercial applications and older facilities. Suitable for smaller three-phase loads mixed with standard 120V single-phase equipment.
480V (277/480V)
The most common voltage for industrial gensets. Powers large motors, industrial chillers, and heavy equipment efficiently. Preferred for manufacturing and heavy commercial applications.
600V (347/600V)
Standard in Canadian industrial applications and some US heavy industrial settings. Higher voltage reduces current requirements and installation costs for large loads.

FAQ
Three-Phase Generator FAQs
What is the difference between single phase and three phase generators?
Single-phase generators produce one AC waveform and are suitable for residential and light commercial loads up to 40 kW. Three-phase generators produce three overlapping AC waveforms (each offset by 120 degrees), delivering continuous, stable power ideal for industrial motors and heavy equipment. Three-phase systems offer 92-94% efficiency versus 85-88% for single-phase, plus 200% motor starting torque and 30-40% longer equipment lifespan due to balanced torque.
How do I calculate what size three-phase generator I need?
Calculate total running wattage of all critical equipment, add 200% for motor starting current, then add 20-30% headroom for future expansion and efficient operation. Remember that three-phase generators typically have a 0.8 power factor—kW output equals kVA rating multiplied by 0.8. For example, a 250 kVA generator delivers 200 kW. Our engineers provide free load analysis to ensure accurate sizing.
What voltage should my three-phase generator be?
480V (277/480V) is the most common voltage for industrial applications in the US and globally. 208V (120/208V) suits light commercial with mixed single-phase loads. 600V (347/600V) is standard in Canada. The voltage must match your facility’s distribution system and equipment nameplates. Many generators feature reconnectable alternators allowing voltage changes by rearranging leads.

Can I run single-phase equipment on a three-phase generator?
Yes, but output capacity drops to approximately 2/3 of rated three-phase power. A 100 kW three-phase generator delivers about 70 kW when powering single-phase loads. Proper load balancing across all three phases is essential—unbalanced loads can overheat one phase and damage the alternator. A qualified electrician should configure connections.
What is the difference between standby and prime power generators?
Standby generators provide emergency backup during grid outages with no overload capability and limited annual runtime (typically 200 hours). Prime power generators operate as the primary power source with unlimited runtime hours and 10% overload capacity for varying loads. Continuous duty generators handle steady, constant loads at 100% rating for extended periods. Selecting the correct duty rating is essential for warranty coverage and equipment longevity.
How long do commercial three-phase generators last?
With proper maintenance, diesel generator sets last 20,000-30,000 hours (25-35 years for standby use, 10-15 years for prime power). Key factors affecting lifespan include load factor (avoid chronic under-loading below 30%), maintenance quality, fuel quality, and environmental conditions.
