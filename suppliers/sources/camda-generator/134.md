Noise pollution point analysis of generators:
1. Exhaust noise
Exhaust noise is a high-temperature, high-speed pulsating airflow noise, which is the most energy-intensive part of engine noise. It is much higher than the intake noise and the mechanical noise of the body radiation, and is the most important component of the total engine noise. Its fundamental frequency is the firing frequency of the engine.
2. Mechanical noise
The mechanical noise is mainly caused by the vibration or mutual impact caused by the cyclic changes of the gas pressure and the inertia of the motion during the operation of the moving parts of the engine. The most serious ones are the following: the noise of the piston crank linkage mechanism, The noise of the valve train, the noise of the transmission gear, and the mechanical vibration and noise caused by the unbalanced inertia force.
3. Combustion noise
Combustion noise is the structural vibration and noise generated by diesel during combustion.
4. Cooling fan and exhaust noise
The fan noise of the unit is composed of eddy current noise and rotating noise. The rotating noise is caused by the periodic disturbance of the blade cutting air flow of the fan. The eddy current noise is generated when the air flow is separated on the rotating blade section, due to the viscosity of the gas. A swirling flow that radiates an unsteady flow noise. Exhaust noise, airflow noise, fan noise, and mechanical noise are radiated through the exhaust passage.
5. Ingress noise
The Diesel Generator Set needs to have enough fresh air supply during normal operation to ensure the normal operation of the engine on the one hand, and to create good heat dissipation conditions for the unit on the other hand, otherwise the unit cannot guarantee its performance. The air intake system of the unit basically includes the air inlet passage and the intake system of the engine itself. The air inlet passage of the unit must enable the fresh air to smoothly enter the engine room. At the same time, the mechanical noise and airflow noise of the unit can also be radiated to the engine room through the air inlet passage. outside.
6. Generator noise
Generator noise includes electromagnetic noise caused by magnetic field pulsations between the stator and the rotor, and mechanical noise generated by the rotation of the rolling bearing.

What Is a 1375 KVA Standby Shock-Proof Diesel Generator?
A 1375 KVA standby shock-proof diesel generator is a large-capacity emergency power system engineered to provide backup electricity when utility power becomes unavailable. Unlike prime power generators that run continuously, standby generators remain ready for operation and automatically start when a power outage occurs.
The shock-proof design refers to enhanced structural protection that reduces the impact of vibration, mechanical movement, and external stress on critical components. This design significantly improves equipment durability, especially in demanding industrial environments.

How the 1375 KVA Standby Shock-Proof Diesel Generator Works
Although the technology inside a modern diesel generator is sophisticated, the operating principle is straightforward.
When utility power is functioning normally, the generator remains in standby mode. The controller continuously monitors incoming electrical supply.
If a power interruption occurs, the ATS immediately detects the failure and sends a startup signal to the diesel engine. The engine quickly reaches operating speed and drives the alternator to generate electrical energy.
The generated power is then transferred to the facility's electrical system, ensuring critical operations continue with minimal interruption.
The process typically follows these steps:
Utility power failure occurs.
Control system detects the outage.
Engine starts automatically.
Alternator generates electricity.
Load transfers to generator power.
Generator supplies backup electricity.
Utility power returns.
Load transfers back to the grid.
Generator enters cooldown mode and shuts down.
This automatic operation protects businesses from costly downtime and production interruptions.
