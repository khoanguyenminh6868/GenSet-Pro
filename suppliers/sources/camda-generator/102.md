100 kW biogas generator – Complete Technical Guide, Applications, Specifications & Industry Overview
1. Introduction to 100 KW biogas generator
The 100 KW Bio Gas Generator is a highly efficient renewable energy system designed to convert biogas produced from organic waste into stable electrical power. It is widely used in agricultural farms, wastewater treatment plants, landfill gas recovery systems, food processing facilities, and small-to-medium industrial energy projects.
As global demand for clean energy continues to rise, the 100 kW bio Gas Generator Set has become an essential solution for sustainable power generation. It transforms organic waste into electricity while reducing greenhouse gas emissions and supporting circular economy development.
This system is engineered for continuous operation, high fuel efficiency, and stable output performance even under variable gas quality conditions.
2. What is a 100 KW Biogas Generator?
A 100 KW biogas generator is a combustion-based power generation system that uses biogas as fuel instead of fossil fuels such as diesel or natural gas.
Biogas is produced through anaerobic digestion of organic materials, including:
Livestock manure
Agricultural residues
Food waste
Sewage sludge
Industrial organic waste
Typical Biogas Composition:
| | Component | Percentage Range
| Methane (CH₄) | 50% – 70%
| Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) | 30% – 50%
| Hydrogen Sulfide (H₂S) | 0–2000 ppm
| Water Vapor | Variable
The generator converts methane energy into mechanical energy via combustion and then into electrical energy through an alternator.
3. Working Principle of 100 KW Biogas Generator
The operation of a 100 kW biogas generator system follows a structured energy conversion process:
Step 1: Biogas Production
Organic waste decomposes in anaerobic digesters, producing methane-rich gas.
Step 2: Gas Cleaning & Purification
Biogas is treated to remove impurities such as:
Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S)
Moisture
Siloxanes (in some industrial applications)
Step 3: Gas Pressure Regulation
Gas pressure is stabilized to ensure consistent engine operation.
Step 4: Combustion Process
Biogas is mixed with air and burned in an internal combustion engine.
Step 5: Power Generation
Mechanical energy drives the alternator, producing 100 kW electrical output .
Step 6: Heat Recovery (Optional CHP System)
Waste heat can be recovered for heating or industrial use.
